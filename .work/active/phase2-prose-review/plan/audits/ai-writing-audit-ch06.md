## 2. Executive assessment

The chapter is technically dense and mostly well grounded. Every headline number I checked is traceable to a dated evidence file, though several are presented with the wrong run label or without a caveat (E1-E6): the 754 retrieval table, the live endpoint row, the DDIA table, the generation harness, the tracing harness, the 4/4 verdict replay, the 572-page timing run, and the 24-row adherence emission. The appendix claim at line 102 is true: the 24 rows and four `decidedAt` values are present in `appendix.tex`, and `ch:appendix` resolves. There is no placeholder, TODO, repeated paragraph, or dangling cross-reference. These are strengths, not authorship evidence.

The dominant stylistic pattern is a metric-first paragraph built from short declaratives and the verb `reads`: "Groundedness reads 0.8667", "Confidence reads 1.0", "The span reads 32 days". The frame occurs 12 times and flattens rates, AUCs, seconds, and days into one verb. Nine paragraphs exceed the six-sentence cap, so the effect is a long checklist of short cells rather than a developing argument. The second pattern is evaluative labelling: "honest terminal", "honest nulls", "real wins", "measured-zero", "clear wins", "The limits are stated plainly". These labels praise the reporting stance instead of describing the result. The third is copied template: line 109 reuses the Phase-I discussion opening verbatim, and line 115 reuses a Ch04 closing sentence verbatim.

The main risks are evidence alignment, not vocabulary. The chapter presents the old 0.0 coding derivability as current even though the fresh pull reports 0.5349 and 3/3 gold agreement; it repeats a cross-user sufficiency range in a per-user table whose caption claims per-user values sit in the JSON; it places the rubric arm's cutoff/margin calibration inside the loop-verdict section; and it places the gate change next to the 4/4 conversion without the evidence's own caveat that confidence 1.0 would also have cleared the old 0.9 gate. The external pyBKT anchor is listed as an ECE note of "gate 0.6" while the model registry marks that cell `not_reportable_high_ece` with ECE 0.2716. These are submission-readiness issues.

The Discussion (6.5) partly earns its place: the transfer paragraph at line 113 and the limits paragraph at line 115 add synthesis. But the first paragraph largely restates numbers already reported, and the "four clear wins" grouping mixes two comparative gains with one absolute level, one n=4 synthetic conversion, and one deployment timing that Section 6.2 explicitly says "never promotes a candidate". The chapter needs interpretation and evidence repair more than it needs synonym variation.

These observations do not establish whether the text was written by a human, an AI, or a combination. They identify writing-quality and evidence-alignment issues only.

## 5. Chapter-level pattern map

**Pattern family A: metric-first short declaratives.** Lines 5, 7, 34, 57, 77, 82, and 102 each open with a summary claim, often a table pointer, then run through short subject-verb-object sentences. Nine paragraphs exceed the six-sentence cap (STE 6.6), which means the paragraphs are long but the sentences are short. The `reads` frame (S1) is the clearest marker. This is repeated across all five sections, not isolated. The highest-value revision is to combine related sentences and add one interpretive sentence per table, not to inflate the vocabulary.

**Pattern family B: copied cross-document sentences.** Line 109 reproduces the Phase-I discussion opening; line 115 reproduces a Ch04 closing sentence. The pattern is isolated in count but high in salience because both sit at section boundaries. A two-phase dissertation can legitimately reuse its own framing, but these two sentences no longer carry new information in Ch06. The highest-value revision is to replace them with Phase-II-specific claims.

**Pattern family C: evaluative labels instead of outcomes.** "Honest terminal" (34), "honest nulls" and "real wins" (109), "measured-zero" (109), "clear wins" (109), "The limits are stated plainly" (115), and "rather than a softened adjective" (109) form one family. The labels are not random: they defend the chapter against the charge of spinning null results. But the defence is already carried by the numbers and the caveats. The density turns a strength into self-praise. Keep at most one label and state the rest as outcomes.

**Pattern family D: evidence-alignment faults.** E1 (stale 0.0), E2 (anchor ECE omitted), E4 (per-user sufficiency), E5 (gate causation), E6 (rubric calibration in 6.3), and E7 (Ch04 overclaim) are the highest-risk findings. They are not stylistic preferences; they change what the chapter claims. E1 and E5 are the most serious because the chapter currently tells a cleaner story than the evidence supports.

**Pattern family E: discussion restatement.** Section 6.5 repeats the tracing, coding, difficulty, adherence, retrieval, generation, verdict, and timing results with the same numbers. The transfer paragraph (113) and the limits paragraph (115) are the main parts that add synthesis. The chapter opening promises "what the numbers mean"; the discussion delivers a summary plus labels. The highest-value revision is to cut the repeated numbers and add a mechanism or alternative for the two main wins: why reranking helps more on DDIA, and why bkt-v1's calibration halves while its ranking stays at parity.

**Pattern family F: term and spelling drift.** `quantisation` and `canonicalises` against `behavior` and `prioritize`; `accoding` against `ACcoding`; `measured zero` against `measured-zero`; `4 of 4` against Ch04's `4 out of 4`. These are copy-editing issues, not argument issues, but they are visible in a chapter that is otherwise precise.

**Pattern family G: specificity as a strength.** The chapter names the corpus sizes, split labels, gates, run counts, and file-level evidence. It does not use generic "significant improvement" language. This is the main reason the audit does not read the chapter as generic. The revision should preserve this density while removing the mechanical frame and the self-praise.

**Highest-value chapter revision:** repair E1, E2, E4, E5, and E6 before any style pass. They affect what the results claim. The next revision is a rewrite of 6.5 around mechanisms and alternatives, not a paraphrase of 6.1-6.4.
