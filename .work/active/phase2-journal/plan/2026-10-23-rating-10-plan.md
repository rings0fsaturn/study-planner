## 1. What 10/10 means

A 10/10 is a writing-quality and submission-readiness rating, not an AI-authorship score.
It is reached only when all seven gates below pass at the same time.

| Gate | Pass condition |
|---|---|
| G1 Evidence | Every headline number traces to one named evidence file and one table. No sentence claims "match" unless every compared metric matches. |
| G2 Consistency | No pair of statements in the paper reports different values for the same cut, or the same value for different cuts. |
| G3 Definitions | Every acronym is expanded at first use. `verdict pair`, `sufficiency`, `hold`/`Keep`, `r@k`, and `MRR` are defined once. |
| G4 Argument | The Introduction states a research question and a gap. The Conclusion states an implication and a limit, not a recap of the abstract. |
| G5 Prose | No P0 or P1 finding remains in a fresh audit run by a separate pass. At most three P2 findings, each explicitly accepted with a reason. |
| G6 Production | LaTeX build: 0 errors, 0 undefined references, 0 overfull boxes. No page has more than 15% unintended whitespace. Every figure is legible at 100% zoom. |
| G7 References | 24/24 citations resolve both directions. Every DOI, arXiv ID, and URL opens. No unverified or future-dated entry remains. |

**Rating scale used by this plan**

| Rating | Priority score | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 10/10 | 0 | No concrete issue in the supplied sample; all seven gates pass. |
| 9/10 | 1 | Minor local issues only. |
| 8/10 | 2 | Repeated issues affecting readability or specificity. |
| 6/10 | 3 | Major issues affecting evidence, consistency, or submission readiness. |

Do not add finding scores into an "AI score". The rating is a review-priority summary.

## 7. Phase 4 - Prose surgery

### 4.1 Introduction

Replace the structure tour (`Methods states... Results reports... The conclusion states...`) with:

1. The problem: fixed plans and logged hours do not measure understanding.
2. The gap: no loop connects graded attempts to a replan decision.
3. The research question: can a gated, evidence-backed loop replan without overclaiming?
4. The contribution: the loop, the four arms, and the frozen-evidence evaluation.

Keep "One-to-one tutoring outperforms group instruction [1]" only if it is tied to the single-learner scope; otherwise cut it.

### 4.2 Conclusion

Rewrite around three sentences:

1. What the loop does that prior planners do not (join grading, tracing, retrieval, and a gate).
2. What the evidence supports (parity on ranking, better calibration, 4/4 conversions) and what it does not (population effects, long-term retention).
3. The next test that would change the claim (a real-learner study), not a numbered recap of the abstract.

Delete the repeated metric list; the abstract and Results already carry it.

### 4.3 Rhythm and template

- Merge at least two fragment clusters per section into one sentence with a subordinate clause. Example: "The plan does not adapt. Logged hours do not prove understanding." -> "The plan does not adapt, and logged hours do not show whether study produced understanding."
- Break the four-beat Literature template once: turn §2.2 or §2.3 into a direct comparison of two sources, not another source-gap-keep entry.
- Remove meta prose: "The section ends with a sourcing note...", "We draw all figures to single-column width", "The verdict table and the dip figure read together."
- Remove false counts: "Three rules set the scope" only if three follow; "The loop runs five stages" only if five are enumerated in the same paragraph.
- Replace rhetorical absolutes with bounded claims: "never changes the grade" is fine as a contract; "never adds a number to results" appears twice, keep one.

Exit: fresh read-through shows varied sentence length, no meta paragraph, no false count.

## 13. Risks

- **Evidence files may not support a clean replacement for E4.** If the per-archetype rebound suff values are not stored, keep the range and add a footnote; do not invent per-user values.
- **Page count may grow past 15pp** after definitions and the intro rewrite. Offset by cutting the meta prose and the repeated conclusion metrics.
- **A fresh audit may find a new issue** introduced by the rewrite. Treat that as a normal iteration, not a failure.
- **The rating can stall at 9/10** if a reference cannot be verified. In that case, remove the claim rather than leave an unverified citation.

## 14. What not to do

- Do not "humanize" the prose or add random synonym variation to lower a detector score.
- Do not change a number to match another table without checking the evidence file first.
- Do not average groundedness and gold support; the paper correctly keeps them separate.
- Do not claim the audit proves authorship either way.
